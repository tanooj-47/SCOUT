## 1. Environmental Data Classification & Typology

Environmental data in SCOUT is categorized strictly by its physical and mathematical origin. Crucially, **Open-Meteo `current` weather is NOT classified as direct physical station observation**. It is model-derived analysis / nowcast fields generated by Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) assimilation and spatial interpolation.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    ENVIRONMENTAL DATA TYPOLOGY                                     |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  │
  ├─► 1. STATION OBSERVATION (STATION_OBSERVED)
  │    Physical sensor measurements recorded by an in-situ meteorological instrument (AWS, ARG, METAR, WMO).
  │    Characteristics: Discrete point coordinates, station identifier, sensor height, potential gaps.
  │    Examples: IMD Automatic Weather Stations, airport METAR reports.
  │
  ├─► 2. MODEL ANALYSIS / NOWCAST (MODEL_ANALYSIS)
  │    Zero-lead-time assimilation fields or short-range spatial nowcasts produced by an NWP model.
  │    Characteristics: Spatially continuous grid, interpolated across terrain, blends physics + radar/sat.
  │    Examples: Open-Meteo `current` block, DWD ICON-D2 nowcast, ECMWF HRES 00Z/12Z analysis.
  │    CRITICAL DISTINCTION: This is NOT a raw station observation. It is a model product.
  │
  ├─► 3. HISTORICAL REANALYSIS (REANALYSIS)
  │    Retrospective physical assimilation combining historical observations with an unchanging numerical model.
  │    Characteristics: Gridded, physically consistent, immutable historical record, latency of days to months.
  │    Examples: ECMWF ERA5 / ERA5-Land (via Open-Meteo Archive), NASA MERRA-2 (via NASA POWER).
  │    CRITICAL DISTINCTION: NASA POWER is model reanalysis, NOT ground-truth physical sensor data.
  │
  ├─► 4. NUMERICAL WEATHER PREDICTION FORECAST (FORECAST)
  │    Forward simulations projecting future atmospheric states at positive lead times (lead_time_hours > 0).
  │    Characteristics: Associated with an issue/run time (init_time_utc), lead time horizon, and model version.
  │    Examples: ECMWF IFS 10-day forecast, NOAA GFS 16-day forecast.
  │
  └─► 5. DERIVED ENVIRONMENTAL INDICES (DERIVED)
       Mathematical indices computed from sequential atmospheric observations.
       Characteristics: Requires documented recursive methodology, source variable provenance, and run history.
       Examples: Canadian FWI System components (FFMC, DMC, DC, ISI, BUI, FWI), Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD).
```

### 1.1 Temporal Provenance Standards
Every observation item records two orthogonal timestamps, with forecast and model records capturing run metadata:
- **`valid_time_utc` (DateTime):** The exact physical moment in UTC when the atmospheric conditions exist or are projected to exist.
- **`retrieved_at_utc` (DateTime):** The exact physical moment in UTC when the SCOUT backend ingested the record.
- **`init_time_utc` (Optional[DateTime]):** The model cycle initialization/run timestamp (applicable to `FORECAST` and `MODEL_ANALYSIS`; `NULL` for station observations and historical reanalysis).
- **`lead_time_hours` (Optional[float]):** The projection horizon ($\text{lead\_time\_hours} = \text{valid\_time\_utc} - \text{init\_time\_utc}$). For analysis $\text{lead\_time} = 0.0$; for forecasts $\text{lead\_time} > 0.0$; for station observations and reanalysis $\text{lead\_time} = \text{None}$.

| Field Name | Description | `STATION_OBSERVED` | `MODEL_ANALYSIS` | `REANALYSIS` | `FORECAST` | `DERIVED` |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

## 2. Refined Environmental Provider Capabilities (Official Documentation)

### 2.1 Open-Meteo API
- **Official Documentation:** [open-meteo.com/en/docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs), [open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api)
- **Current Conditions Provenance:** **Verified Fact.** Derived from high-frequency Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) model analyses and spatial nowcasts (e.g. DWD ICON-D2 nowcast, ECMWF HRES analysis, NOAA GFS analysis); **not** direct physical weather station sensor feeds.
- **Forecast Coverage & Models:** **Verified Fact.** Global coverage via ECMWF IFS (~9 km / 0.1°), NOAA GFS (~13 km / 0.25°), DWD ICON (~13 km). Hourly projections up to 16 days.
- **Historical Reanalysis:** **Verified Fact.** ECMWF ERA5 (~31 km / 0.25°) and ERA5-Land (~9 km / 0.1°) from 1940 to present. Latency: 2 to 5 days.
- **Variables & Units:** **Verified Fact.** Temperature at 2m (`temperature_2m`), relative humidity at 2m (`relative_humidity_2m`), wind speed at 10m (`wind_speed_10m`), wind direction at 10m (`wind_direction_10m`), precipitation (`precipitation`), surface pressure (`surface_pressure`). Units can be requested directly in SI (`&wind_speed_unit=ms`).
- **Licensing & Rate Limits:** **Verified Fact.** Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0). Requires attribution: "Weather data by Open-Meteo.com". Rate limits: 10,000 calls/day, 5,000 calls/hour, 600 calls/minute. Zero credentials required.

### 2.2 NASA POWER API
- **Official Documentation:** [power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/), [power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/)
- **Hourly Endpoint:** `https://power.larc.nasa.gov/api/temporal/hourly/point`
- **Daily Endpoint:** `https://power.larc.nasa.gov/api/temporal/daily/point`
- **Data Provenance:** **Verified Fact.** Sourced from NASA's GMAO MERRA-2 assimilation model and CERES satellite processing. **It is reanalysis/assimilation, NOT ground-truth physical station data.**
- **Temporal Coverage Distinctions & Parameter Qualifications:** **Verified Fact.**
  - **Daily API:** Available from **1981-01-01 to present** for meteorology (MERRA-2).
  - **Hourly API:** Available from **2001-01-01 to present** for unified API queries (coinciding with the start of the satellite CERES record).
  - **Parameter Coverage Differences:** While MERRA-2 atmospheric assimilation exists from 1981, NASA POWER's public API endpoints package meteorological and solar variables together; queries requesting parameters before 2001 on the hourly endpoint fail. Client adapters must validate requested date ranges against parameter availability.
- **Time Standard Behavior:** **Verified Fact.** The API defaults to **Local Solar Time (`time-standard=LST`)**. To align with SCOUT's UTC database standard, the adapter **must explicitly specify `&time-standard=UTC`** in every request.
- **Data Latency:** **Verified Fact.** 2 to 3 days latency for meteorological variables (`T2M`, `RH2M`, `WS10M`); 5 to 7 days for solar variables. Zero forecast support.
- **Variables & Units:** **Verified Fact.** `T2M` (°C), `RH2M` (%), `WS10M` (m/s), `WD10M` (degrees), `PRECTOTCORR` (corrected precipitation, mm/hr or mm/day), `PS` (surface pressure in **kPa** — requires conversion to hPa: $\times 10.0$).
- **Rate Limits:** **Verified Fact.** Open public REST API (no key). Max 15 parameters per hourly query, max 20 per daily query. Soft rate limiting on aggressive loops.

## 3. Database Row Granularity & Migration-Ready Identity Design

### 3.1 Granularity Decision: Alternative A (Observation Bundle)
- **Alternative A (Observation Bundle — Recommended):** One row represents the co-located atmospheric state at a specific location and valid time (`temperature_celsius`, `relative_humidity`, `wind_speed_mps`, `wind_direction_degrees`, `precipitation_mm`, `surface_pressure_hpa`, `metrics_payload`).
  - **Foreign Key Preservation:** Preserves 100% compatibility with `hazard_predictions.primary_observation_id: UUID`, which points directly to the atmospheric state snapshot initializing a wildfire or flood simulation.
  - **Query Performance:** Enables $O(1)$ multi-variable retrieval (`SELECT * FROM environmental_observations WHERE ...`) without requiring 4–6 table self-joins.
  - **Storage Efficiency:** Generates 1 row per hourly timestep rather than 6–10 rows.
  - **Consistency:** Ensures atomic transaction commits of complete atmospheric states.
- **Alternative B (Single Variable per Row / EAV Narrow Table — Rejected):** Breaks `hazard_predictions.primary_observation_id`, multiplies row count by 6x–10x, and introduces heavy join overhead.

### 3.2 Rigorous Grid-Cell & Location Identity Architecture
We reject arbitrary coordinate rounding formulas (e.g. `grid:open_meteo:ecmwf_ifs:0.1:286_772`) because client-side rounding does not define actual NWP grid origins, alignments, or projections.

The location identity is structured as follows:
1. **Preserve Requested and Resolved Coordinates:**
   - `requested_latitude: Float` and `requested_longitude: Float`: The exact point requested by the client.
   - `resolved_latitude: Float` and `resolved_longitude: Float`: The actual grid-center or station coordinates returned in the provider's API response metadata.
   - `location: Geometry(Point, 4326)`: PostGIS spatial geometry column set to `ST_SetSRID(ST_MakePoint(resolved_longitude, resolved_latitude), 4326)`.
2. **Deterministic `location_type` Discriminator:**
   - `'station'`: In-situ ground station.
   - `'grid'`: Provider-resolved numerical model grid cell.
   - `'incident'`: Point location tied strictly to an incident where no provider grid is resolved.
   - `'point'`: Arbitrary ungridded coordinates.
3. **Canonical Namespaced `location_id`:**
   - **For Stations:** `station:{provider}:{station_code}` (e.g. `station:imd:VIDP`, `station:wmo:42182`).
   - **For Gridded Products:** If the provider returns an explicit grid ID, it is used. Otherwise, format the documented provider-resolved coordinates to 4 decimal places (~11m precision):
     `grid:{provider}:{model_or_dataset}:{resolved_lat_4dp}_{resolved_lon_4dp}` (e.g. `grid:open_meteo:ecmwf_ifs:28.6200_77.2200`).
   - **For Incidents:** `incident:{incident_id}` (e.g. `incident:3fa85f64-5717-4562-b3fc-2c963f66afa6`).
   - **For Arbitrary Points:** `point:{lat_4dp}_{lon_4dp}`.
4. **Relational Incident Linking:**
   - Dedicated column `incident_id: UUID, nullable=True, ForeignKey("incidents.id", ondelete="RESTRICT")`.
   - If two incidents request weather at locations resolving to the same provider grid cell, both reference the same physical weather row, or have incident-specific rows linked to that common `location_id`.

### 3.3 Migration-Ready PostgreSQL Constraints (Partial Unique Indexes)

To prevent collisions between providers, models, or forecast cycles without relying on unsupported table-level expressions:

```sql
-- 1. In-situ Station Observations and Historical Reanalysis (Immutable Valid Time)
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX uq_env_obs_ground_and_reanalysis
ON environmental_observations (
    location_type,
    location_id,
    provider,
    dataset_or_model,
    typology,
    valid_time_utc
)
WHERE typology IN ('station_observed', 'reanalysis');

-- 2. Model Analysis / Nowcasts (Preserves Distinct Analysis Cycles When Available)
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX uq_env_obs_model_analysis
ON environmental_observations (
    location_type,
    location_id,
    provider,
    dataset_or_model,
    valid_time_utc,
    COALESCE(init_time_utc, '1970-01-01 00:00:00+00'::timestamptz)
)
WHERE typology = 'model_analysis';

-- 3. Numerical Weather Prediction Forecasts (Full Cycle, Horizon & Valid Time Consistency)
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX uq_env_obs_forecast
ON environmental_observations (
    location_type,
    location_id,
    provider,
    dataset_or_model,
    valid_time_utc,
    init_time_utc,
    lead_time_hours
)
WHERE typology = 'forecast';

-- 4. Derived Environmental Indices (Algorithm Version & Input Provenance Governance)
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX uq_env_obs_derived
ON environmental_observations (
    location_type,
    location_id,
    algorithm_name,
    algorithm_version,
    valid_time_utc,
    source_fingerprint
)
WHERE typology = 'derived';
```

#### Why `provider`, `dataset_or_model`, and `valid_time_utc` are Retained in `uq_env_obs_forecast`:
1. **Multi-Model Disambiguation:** Prevents collision between ECMWF IFS and NOAA GFS (or between Open-Meteo and IMD) projecting weather for the same location and time.
2. **Query Performance:** Queries from endpoints, services, and ML models filter primarily on `valid_time_utc` (e.g. "retrieve forecast valid for tomorrow at 12:00 UTC"). Storing and indexing `valid_time_utc` directly enables index-only scans and fast B-tree range queries without runtime addition (`init_time_utc + lead_time_hours`).
3. **Discretization & Physical Consistency:** Models may output lead times in fractional hours or sub-hourly steps, or valid times may represent time-averaged accumulation periods. Retaining `valid_time_utc` enforces absolute physical time consistency.

#### Database Validation Constraints:
```sql
ALTER TABLE environmental_observations
ADD CONSTRAINT ck_env_obs_forecast_metadata
CHECK (
    typology != 'forecast' OR (
        init_time_utc IS NOT NULL 
        AND lead_time_hours >= 0.0
        AND valid_time_utc = init_time_utc + (lead_time_hours * INTERVAL '1 hour')
    )
);

ALTER TABLE environmental_observations
ADD CONSTRAINT ck_env_obs_derived_metadata
CHECK (
    typology != 'derived' OR (
        algorithm_name IS NOT NULL 
        AND algorithm_version IS NOT NULL 
        AND source_fingerprint IS NOT NULL
    )
);
```

### 3.4 Concrete Insert & Conflict Scenarios

1. **Scenario 1: Station Telemetry Re-Ingestion (Idempotent Retry)**
   - Initial insert: IMD AWS station `VIDP`, `valid_time='2026-10-31 12:00:00Z'`.
   - Subsequent pipeline run triggers `uq_env_obs_ground_and_reanalysis` conflict. Handled via `ON CONFLICT (...) DO UPDATE SET retrieved_at_utc = EXCLUDED.retrieved_at_utc`. 0 duplicate rows created.
2. **Scenario 2: Historical Reanalysis Grid Ingestion (Immutable Record)**
   - Initial insert: ERA5-Land reanalysis for New Delhi grid cell, `valid_time='2026-05-15 12:00:00Z'`, `typology='reanalysis'`. Subsequent fetch conflicts on `uq_env_obs_ground_and_reanalysis`; updates retrieval timestamp without duplicating historical data.
3. **Scenario 3: Successive Forecast Cycles (Model Drift & Evolution Preserved)**
   - 00Z Run: ECMWF IFS forecast issued at `init_time='2026-10-31 00:00:00Z'`, `lead_time=24.0`, `valid_time='2026-11-01 00:00:00Z'`.
   - 12Z Run: ECMWF IFS forecast issued at `init_time='2026-10-31 12:00:00Z'`, `lead_time=12.0`, `valid_time='2026-11-01 00:00:00Z'`.
   - Result: `init_time_utc` and `lead_time_hours` differ. **Zero conflict.** Both forecast runs are preserved side-by-side in `uq_env_obs_forecast` for accuracy and drift analysis.
4. **Scenario 4: Two Incidents Sharing the Same Resolved Physical Grid Cell**
   - Incident 1 at $(28.6140, 77.2092)$ and Incident 2 at $(28.6145, 77.2098)$ both query Open-Meteo ECMWF IFS.
   - Provider returns resolved grid coordinates $(28.6200, 77.2200)$ for both.
   - Shared location identity is `location_id = "grid:open_meteo:ecmwf_ifs:28.6200_77.2200"`.
   - The weather observation is stored once for that grid cell and valid hour. Both incidents link to or reference this shared physical observation without duplicate database storage.
5. **Scenario 5: Reanalysis vs. Forecast at Same Valid Time (Zero Collision)**
   - Reanalysis record valid at `2026-10-01 12:00:00Z` lives in `uq_env_obs_ground_and_reanalysis`; forecast record valid at the same time lives in `uq_env_obs_forecast`. Disjoint partial index predicates guarantee they can never collide or overwrite each other.

## 4. Canadian Forest Fire Weather Index (FWI) Scientific Design & Verification

### 4.1 Authoritative Technical References & 49-Day Benchmark
- **Primary Source:** C.E. Van Wagner and T.L. Pickett (1985), *Equations and FORTRAN Program for the Canadian Forest Fire Weather Index System*, Forestry Technical Report 33 (FTR 33), Canadian Forestry Service, Petawawa National Forestry Institute, Chalk River, Ontario.
- **49-Day Benchmark Dataset:** Documented in Appendix 2 of FTR 33 ("Sample Run and Output"). Spans 49 consecutive daily observations (April 13 to May 31, 1985) at latitude 40.0°N.
- **Standard Benchmark Parameters:**
  - Standard initial values: $\text{FFMC}_0 = 85.0$, $\text{DMC}_0 = 6.0$, $\text{DC}_0 = 15.0$.
  - Day 1 (April 13): `lat=40.0, temp=17.0, rh=42.0, ws=25.0, prec=0.0`. Published output: `FFMC=87.7, DMC=8.5, DC=20.0, ISI=8.7, BUI=8.9, FWI=8.4`.
  - Day 49 (May 31): Terminal values following rain wetting and extended drying spells.
- **Licensing & Redistribution Statement:** FTR 33 was published under Canadian Crown Copyright (Minister of Supply and Services Canada 1985). Formal reuse and redistribution permissions must be verified before copying the raw sample dataset directly into the SCOUT repository as a test fixture. Alternatively, independently open-licensed test vectors (such as from the GPL-licensed CRAN package `cffdrs`) or synthetic test cases generated from the published equations with documented floating-point tolerance ($\pm 0.1$) will be used.

### 4.2 Non-Linear Daily DC Equations vs. Exponential Convergence
We reject the static exponential approximation ($e^{-30/52} = 56.1\%$ error remaining or 120-day convergence) as scientifically unjustified:
- The Drought Code (DC) does not follow a universal exponential decay constant. It updates daily through non-linear empirical equations:
  1. **Effective Rainfall & Moisture Depletion:** If daily rain $r_0 > 2.8\,\text{mm}$, effective rainfall is $r_d = 0.83 r_0 - 1.27$. The moisture equivalent $Q_{t-1} = 800 \exp(-\text{DC}_{t-1}/400)$ is replenished: $Q_t = Q_{t-1} + 3.937 r_d$, and $\text{DC}' = 400 \ln(800 / Q_t)$.
  2. **Potential Evapotranspiration & Drying:** Daily drying factor $V = 0.36 T + L_f$, where $L_f$ is the month- and latitude-dependent daylength factor.
  3. **Daily Update:** $\text{DC}_t = \text{DC}' + 0.5 V$.
- **Convergence Reality:** The actual rate of convergence toward true duff moisture depends heavily on temperature, daylength, relative humidity, and rainfall events. A single substantial rainfall event ($>20\,\text{mm}$) can saturate the duff and reset the Drought Code, whereas prolonged hot, dry drought conditions lead to slow equilibration.
- The effect of initialization must be evaluated by running the actual daily equations over representative empirical weather sequences, NOT by assuming a static exponential constant. Universal spin-up durations will not be chosen in Phase 0; Indian seasonal initialization remains a separate research problem.

### 4.3 The Four-Layer Scientific Distinction
1. **Layer 1: Mathematical Correctness:** Algorithm reproduces the Van Wagner & Pickett (1985) benchmark test vectors within documented floating-point tolerance ($\pm 0.1$).
2. **Layer 2: Data-Input Suitability:** Inputs strictly satisfy solar noon observations (12:00/13:00 LST) and 24-hr noon-to-noon cumulative rainfall.
3. **Layer 3: Regional Calibration:** Danger rating cutoffs (Low–Extreme) must be calibrated against historical Indian forest fires (e.g. Forest Survey of India satellite fire points), not Canadian boreal pine thresholds.
4. **Layer 4: Operational Validation:** Must be validated against real fire behavior before use in operational civil defense.

**Implementation Policy:** FWI will be labeled `experimental_uncalibrated_fwi`, deferred to a separately approved scientific subphase, and accompanied by mandatory operational disclaimers.

## 5. Reliability, Fault Tolerance & Layered Caching Design

### 5.1 Deterministic Cache Key Formulation
```
scout:env_cache:{provider}:{model_or_dataset}:{typology}:{location_key}:{time_window_or_run}:{variables_hash}
```
- `provider`: `open_meteo`, `nasa_power`.
- `model_or_dataset`: `ecmwf_ifs`, `era5_land`, `merra2`.
- `typology`: `model_analysis`, `forecast`, `reanalysis`, `station_observed`, `derived`.
- `location_key`: `station:{id}` or `grid:{provider}:{model}:{res_lat}_{res_lon}`.
- `time_window_or_run`:
  - For `model_analysis`: `valid_hour` (e.g. `2026103113`).
  - For `forecast`: `run_{init_time}_step_{lead_hours}`.
  - For `reanalysis`: `window_{start_date}_{end_date}`.
- `variables_hash`: MD5 hash of requested variables list.

### 5.2 Layered TTL Policies & Stale Fallback
- `MODEL_ANALYSIS` (Current): 15–30 minutes TTL.
- `FORECAST`: 2 hours TTL (aligns with NWP 4x daily cycles).
- `REANALYSIS`: 24 hours to indefinite TTL (immutable historical data).
- `DERIVED (FWI)`: 6 hours TTL.
- **Stale Fallback:** On provider failure or timeout, the service serves expired cache entries marked explicitly:
  ```json
  "quality_flags": {
    "is_stale": true,
    "stale_duration_seconds": 1840,
    "fallback_reason": "Open-Meteo gateway timeout (5.0s exceeded)"
  }
  ```
  Missing values are **never** fabricated or zero-filled.
